LANTERN FIELD NOTES - POCKET FIELD GUIDE
========================================

Synthetic demo download. Everything below is invented for a software demo.

1. BEFORE YOU GO
----------------
- Pick a route you have walked in daylight at least twice.
- Charge or fill the lantern. A dim lantern is better than a dead one.
- Tell someone at home which route you chose and when you expect to be back.
- Bring a pencil. Pens skip when the paper is damp.

2. WHAT TO CARRY
----------------
- One small lantern, shaded on the side that faces your eyes.
- A notebook that fits in a coat pocket.
- A spare layer. Evenings by the river are colder than they look.
- Nothing you would mind getting muddy.

3. HOW TO WRITE AN ENTRY
------------------------
Every entry has three lines and no more:

  SAW:     one thing the lantern showed you.
  HEARD:   one sound you could not see the source of.
  GUESSED: one small story that might explain either.

Sign the entry with the colour of your lantern, not your name.
Describe places loosely: "past the second stile" rather than a map
reference. The notebook is about noticing, not about tracking.

4. LANTERN COLOURS USED IN THE NOTEBOOK
---------------------------------------
  amber  - the default; warm and easy on night vision
  green  - for walkers who mostly follow water
  blue   - for walkers who mostly look up
  white  - for first walks; swap it for a colour later

5. LEAVING NO TRACE
-------------------
- Stay on paths. Meadows recover slowly from shortcuts.
- Do not tie ribbons or markers. If you find one, note it and leave it.
- Keep the lantern low near nesting areas and water.
- Take home everything you brought, including spent candles.

6. SHARING ENTRIES
------------------
Copy your three lines onto a card and drop it in the notebook box, or
send them to notes@example.org (a placeholder address for this demo).
Entries are added to the site in batches, never with names attached.

7. QUIET HOURS
--------------
The notebook asks walkers to finish by an hour after full dark. The point
is to see the edge of the evening, not to be out in the middle of the
night.

-- end of field guide --
